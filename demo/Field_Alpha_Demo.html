Analyst decision

Keep the growth assumption. Track when players buy.

Two players continued to wait for a discount. One cited price or budget. The other lacked time to play. A lower price might address one reason. A follow-up should ask whether each player actually bought, when, and at what price.

Five self-selected US adult console owners. The sample cannot establish a price effect, total launch sales or a new price target.

Sources and method

This offline case uses a completed Flashpoint survey export and a reconstructed FinRobot valuation. The team joined the two analyses after collection. Controls recalculate the selected case locally.

  • Flashpoint, Video game purchase plans, export dated Nov 21, 03:47 UTC. Use unique COMPLETE records marked human/open, without a quality exclusion, meeting both screens and completed before cutoff. The export contains 53 incomplete and 12 screened-out records. The fifth eligible completion was recorded on Nov 18 at 23:49:36 New York time.
  • Q3 captured the earlier plan. Q5 captured prior awareness before the price screen. Q6 captured the plan at the listed prices. Q7 asked for the main reason. No one selected Q3 “planned to pre-order or buy during launch week.” The share of this subgroup switching to a discount is undefined.
  • Take-Two interim filing and annual filing; daily closing prices from Twelve Data.
  • FinRobot valuation code. For each closing price, we ran FinRobot’s seed_dcf_inputs, calculate_dcf and solve_for_implied_growth operators. This page uses the saved inputs and reruns the reverse calculation in your browser. The 16.6% base EBITDA margin uses FinRobot’s broad-market fallback after recent losses. Treating development outlays as fixed investment instead gave non-positive steady-state cash flow.
