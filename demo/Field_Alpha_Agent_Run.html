One question, three survey rounds

Explore the original agent decisions, response counts and valuation scenarios.

    The one objective we gave the agent

    “Given Take-Two's $210 share price, how much revenue must GTA VI generate to justify the growth rate implied by the stock, and is TTWO cheap, fairly priced, or expensive?”

    Nothing else was specified. The agent chose the survey questions, the audience, the follow-up rounds and the valuation cases.

    Before asking anyone

    How the agent set itself up

    Read this first

    Most answers in this run come from the synthetic panel, and rounds 2 and 3 each received one completed email response. The numbers show how the agent reasons from round to round. They are too few, and too synthetic, to stand as a market forecast.

    What the agent came back with

    Expensive at $210

    To justify $210 a share, FinRobot's reverse DCF says Take-Two's total revenue has to grow 19.32% a year for ten years, from $6.41B to $37.48B. That is $102.62B more revenue over the decade than the company's path without a GTA VI step-change.

    Inputs: FY2026 and Q2 FY2027 filings, 16.56% EBITDA margin, 8.69% discount rate, 3.0% terminal growth.

    Value per share when the survey answers drive the GTA VI revenue case

    Every survey-based case lands below the share price

    The three survey cases differ in units sold over three years, spend per buyer and margin. Full bridge: evidence/gta6_revenue_bridge.json and evidence/finrobot_valuation_output.json.

    How the three rounds fed the valuation

    • Round 1 set the starting point: few launch buyers, most price ceilings under $70.
    • Round 2 showed the waiting is firm, and that people would pay for story content but not for a subscription on its own.
    • Round 3 turned that into dollars: about $86 per buyer over three years, and half of respondents spending nothing after year two.

    Limits

    Every completed response in round 1, and eleven of the twelve in rounds 2 and 3, come from synthetic personas. Rounds 2 and 3 each received one completed email response. So this is a demonstration of the method. A real study would put the same three rounds in front of a recruited panel of console owners.

    Reuse the workflow

    Run the next company brief

    Change the research question, target audience and ticker with its filing inputs. Review the stopping rule with the analyst.

    Why someone would pay for this

    After a price change, a launch or a controversy, the share price moves at once, but reported sales arrive with the next earnings release. An analyst covering the stock has to take a view in between.

    In this run the agent designed, fielded and read three survey rounds in about 16 minutes, and the whole run cost 11.35 USDC. What limits a real study is how fast people answer, and that is the part Flashpoint's panels supply.

    Where we think it fits

    • Buy-side and sell-side analysts covering consumer names, around events.
    • Corporate strategy and pricing teams testing a price or bundle before committing.
    • Flashpoint's business customers, as a packaged workflow: a question goes in, evidence and a decision come out.

    What we would build next

    • Field the same rounds to a recruited Flashpoint panel of console owners.
    • Have the agent wait for a set number of human answers before it writes the next round.
    • Field the Netflix draft and evaluate its answers in a second full study.

    NFLX · unpublished draft · 0.50 USDC

    Would a $2 increase change a subscriber’s plan?

    One fresh tool_survey call produced two screeners and two decision questions from the Netflix brief alone. No invitations or responses. No Netflix valuation was run.

    S1 · Do you currently have an active Netflix subscription that you personally pay for?
    1. Yes
    2. No
    S2 · Which of the following best describes the Netflix plan you are currently paying for?
    1. Standard with Ads (lower monthly price, includes advertisements)
    2. Standard (no ads)
    3. Premium (no ads, supports more simultaneous streams)
    4. I am not sure which plan I have
    Q1 · Imagine — hypothetically — that the monthly price of your current ad-free Netflix plan increased by $2. No such price change has been announced; this is a scenario question only. Which of the following would you most likely do?
    1. Keep my current ad-free plan at the new, higher price
    2. Switch to a lower-cost Netflix plan that includes ads
    3. Cancel my Netflix subscription entirely
    4. Pause my subscription temporarily and reassess
    5. Not sure — it would depend on other factors at the time
    Q2 · Which single factor would most influence your decision in that hypothetical scenario?
    1. The dollar amount of the increase relative to what I already pay
    2. My personal tolerance for watching advertisements
    3. The quality and availability of Netflix content at that time
    4. Whether competing streaming services offer better value
    5. My overall household budget and other subscription costs
    6. Something else or no single factor stands out

    Draft check: 0 blockers, 0 errors. Before fielding, verify age and US residency at recruitment or add an eligibility question. The saved screeners check payment and plan type.
