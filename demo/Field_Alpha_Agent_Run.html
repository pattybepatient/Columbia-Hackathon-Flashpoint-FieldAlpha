    The one objective we gave the agent

    “Given Take-Two's $210 share price, how much revenue must GTA VI generate to justify the growth rate implied by the stock, and is TTWO cheap, fairly priced, or expensive?”

    Nothing else was specified. The agent chose the survey questions, the audience, the follow-up rounds and the valuation cases.

    What the agent came back with

    Expensive at $210

    To justify $210 a share, FinRobot's reverse DCF says Take-Two's total revenue has to grow 19.32% a year for ten years, from $6.41B to $37.48B. That is $102.62B more revenue over the decade than the company's path without a GTA VI step-change.

    Inputs: FY2026 and Q2 FY2027 filings, 16.56% EBITDA margin, 8.69% discount rate, 3.0% terminal growth.

    How the three rounds fed the valuation

    • Round 1 set the starting point: few launch buyers, most price ceilings under $70.
    • Round 2 showed the waiting is firm, and that people would pay for story content but not for a subscription on its own.
    • Round 3 turned that into dollars: about $86 per buyer over three years, and half of respondents spending nothing after year two.

    Limits

    Every completed response in round 1, and eleven of the twelve in rounds 2 and 3, come from synthetic personas. Rounds 2 and 3 each received one completed email response. So this is a demonstration of the method. A real study would put the same three rounds in front of a recruited panel of console owners.

    The repeatable procedure

    The same six steps work for any consumer company

    1. One question in. An analyst states the decision they need to make about a company.
    2. The agent writes the survey. It picks the audience and four to seven questions that map to revenue drivers, and Flashpoint checks the logic.
    3. The agent fields it. Flashpoint emails unique links to a consented list, or runs a panel.
    4. The agent reads the answers and decides what is still unclear. It writes the next round around those gaps. In this run it did so twice.
    5. Answers become model inputs. Conversion, price ceilings and spend per buyer go into FinRobot's DCF and reverse DCF.
    6. A verdict with its evidence. The output is a call, the cases behind it, and a log of every survey, payment and decision.

    To point it at another company, change the question and the filing inputs (finrobot_analyze.py seed-sec pulls them for a US ticker).

    Why someone would pay for this

    After a price change, a launch or a controversy, the share price moves at once, but reported sales arrive with the next earnings release. An analyst covering the stock has to take a view in between.

    In this run the agent designed, fielded and read three survey rounds in about 16 minutes, and the whole run cost 11.35 USDC. What limits a real study is how fast people answer, and that is the part Flashpoint's panels supply.

    Where we think it fits

    • Buy-side and sell-side analysts covering consumer names, around events.
    • Corporate strategy and pricing teams testing a price or bundle before committing.
    • Flashpoint's business customers, as a packaged workflow: a question goes in, evidence and a decision come out.

    What we would build next

    • Field the same rounds to a recruited Flashpoint panel of console owners.
    • Have the agent wait for a set number of human answers before it writes the next round.
    • Run it on a second company to show the steps carry over.
