        What the agent came back with

        $210 needs a ~33% EBITDA margin

        Even with the survey’s bull-case demand, the price only works if Take-Two earns about a 33% EBITDA margin for ten years, twice the model’s normalized 16.56%. That is Activision Blizzard’s 2012–2021 average. Take-Two’s best year on the same basis was 21%, and GTA V’s launch year was 18%. Verdict: expensive, unless GTA VI makes Take-Two as profitable as Activision Blizzard at its peak, for a decade. The disagreement is about margin, and the survey narrows the demand side.

        Every survey-based case lands below the share price

        Bull −31% · Base −55% · Bear −64% vs. $210. Bridge: evidence/gta6_revenue_bridge.json and evidence/finrobot_valuation_output.json.

        Break-even margin · the EBITDA margin at which each case is worth $210

        Survey demand lowers the hurdle by only 3 to 7 points

        Model’s normalized margin 16.56%Highest margin FinRobot ran: 28%

        Method: FinRobot ran margins from 14.56% to 28%, and its value per share rises in a straight line across them (+$6.01 per point), as it should when the revenue path is fixed. The break-evens above 28% extrapolate that line; they are not separate model runs. Scaling each case’s GTA VI increment with margin slightly flatters the survey cases, so read these break-evens as lower bounds.

        Is 33% realistic? · what game publishers have actually earned

        Take-Two has never been above 21%. Only Activision Blizzard ran at 33%

        $210 break-even, survey bull case: 32.9%

        Same basis for every company: (operating income + depreciation + amortization of acquired intangibles) ÷ revenue, from each 10-K in SEC XBRL data. Take-Two’s FY2023–25 loss years with Zynga impairments are left out of “best year”. This basis is not FinRobot’s normalized 16.56% input (Take-Two FY2026 is 11.8% here), and it runs a few points lower, so the comparable break-even would be a little lower too: still above anything Take-Two has posted. Data: evidence/margin_anchors.json.

        NFLX · unpublished draft · 0.50 USDC

        Would a $2 increase change a subscriber’s plan?

        One fresh tool_survey call produced two screeners and two decision questions from the Netflix brief alone. No invitations or responses. No Netflix valuation was run.

        S1 · Do you currently have an active Netflix subscription that you personally pay for?
        1. Yes
        2. No
        S2 · Which of the following best describes the Netflix plan you are currently paying for?
        1. Standard with Ads (lower monthly price, includes advertisements)
        2. Standard (no ads)
        3. Premium (no ads, supports more simultaneous streams)
        4. I am not sure which plan I have
        Q1 · Imagine — hypothetically — that the monthly price of your current ad-free Netflix plan increased by $2. No such price change has been announced; this is a scenario question only. Which of the following would you most likely do?
        1. Keep my current ad-free plan at the new, higher price
        2. Switch to a lower-cost Netflix plan that includes ads
        3. Cancel my Netflix subscription entirely
        4. Pause my subscription temporarily and reassess
        5. Not sure — it would depend on other factors at the time
        Q2 · Which single factor would most influence your decision in that hypothetical scenario?
        1. The dollar amount of the increase relative to what I already pay
        2. My personal tolerance for watching advertisements
        3. The quality and availability of Netflix content at that time
        4. Whether competing streaming services offer better value
        5. My overall household budget and other subscription costs
        6. Something else or no single factor stands out

        Draft check: 0 blockers, 0 errors, 1 warning (no quota set, expected for an unpublished draft). Before fielding, verify age and US residency at recruitment or add an eligibility question. The saved screeners check payment and plan type.
